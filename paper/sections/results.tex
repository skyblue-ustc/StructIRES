\section{Results}

\subsection{Dataset composition reveals a strong assay and length imbalance}
The assembled IRES-AI identification set contains 46,774 unique sequences, of which 9,172 are
positive and 37,602 are negative. Of these, 44,641 sequences are exactly 174 nt.
Figure~\ref{fig:structires-classifier} shows the validation-clean classifier protocol used for
the controlled structure-fusion comparison.

The released long-form split is not a mutually exclusive ten-fold partition: 16,176 sequences
never occur in a test set and 12,294 occur in two or more test sets. We therefore separate external
published values from a deterministic reconstructed-fold pilot and reserve similarity-cluster
evaluation for the locked benchmark.

\subsection{Identification benchmark}
On reconstructed nested folds, composition features already achieved AUC $0.710\pm0.009$ and AUPR
$0.405\pm0.015$, while hashed 3--6-mer features achieved AUC $0.723\pm0.011$ and AUPR
$0.452\pm0.016$. These are shortcut-control pilots rather than claims against published models.
Published reference values and project runs remain visibly separated in
Table~\ref{tab:prediction} and Figure~\ref{fig:prediction-pilot}.

We independently recomputed the released ten-fold CSV aggregates and recovered RNA-FM
AUC/AUPR/F1 of 0.779/0.615/0.506 and UTR-LM values of 0.765/0.581/0.493. We then executed all ten
released UTR-LM checkpoints on their native test folds and obtained 0.764938/0.580823/0.492983;
the maximum fold-level absolute deviation from the released CSV was $3.74\times10^{-6}$. The first
verified released RNA-FM checkpoint was then rerun on its 4,678-record native test fold, giving
AUC 0.777114, AUPR 0.594668 and F1 0.524311. After size, SHA-256 and deserialization checks, we
executed all ten released checkpoints separately. Their final repeated-holdout mean and sample
standard deviation were 0.778420$\pm$0.009106, 0.614020$\pm$0.013919 and
0.509881$\pm$0.022686 for AUC, AUPR and F1, respectively. Accuracy, sensitivity, specificity,
MCC and ECE were 0.771612$\pm$0.040410, 0.600109$\pm$0.054448,
0.813427$\pm$0.062923, 0.377099$\pm$0.036561 and 0.219028$\pm$0.048236. These
standard deviations are descriptive because the released test holdouts overlap. Source inspection
further showed that both
released training scripts evaluate the test set after every epoch and select the saved checkpoint
using test AUPR. We therefore label these values as released-protocol references rather than
independent blind-test estimates; corrected reruns reserve validation data for model selection.

We next trained the author-style RNA-FM topology directly from the public t12 prior, reserving a
stratified subset of each source-training fold for epoch and threshold selection.  Under this
validation-clean protocol, 	extsc{StructIRES} adds only a sparse ViennaRNA MFE base-pair encoder
and a gated residual to an otherwise matched full RNA-FM fine-tune.  Across three independently
run native folds (0, 2 and 4), the sequence-only re-train reached AUC/AUPR/F1/MCC of
$0.770\pm0.005$/$0.600\pm0.018$/$0.521\pm0.016$/$0.426\pm0.012$, whereas the contact-fusion
model reached $0.774\pm0.007$/$0.601\pm0.022$/$0.533\pm0.024$/$0.439\pm0.047$.
The foldwise mean changes are therefore +0.004 AUC, +0.002 AUPR, +0.013 F1 and +0.013 MCC; ECE
increased by 0.043.  These overlapping native holdouts provide a controlled recognition ablation,
not a claim of independent biological validation or calibration improvement.

We next locked a length-174, 90\% identity-component-disjoint 70/15/15 split before final testing.
The full RNA-FM and BPP/MFE profile gate were selected only from a seed-42 validation comparison;
we then evaluated three fixed seeds once on the untouched test partition.  The sequence-only model
achieved AUC/AUPR/F1 of $0.559\pm0.004$/$0.188\pm0.005$/$0.295\pm0.002$.  The profile fusion
obtained $0.557\pm0.017$/$0.183\pm0.003$/$0.297\pm0.010$.  Thus its small mean F1 change
(+0.002) was accompanied by lower AUC (-0.002) and AUPR (-0.005), with substantial seed variation.
We retain this as a negative strict-split ablation rather than claiming that the global
ViennaRNA-profile gate improves IRES recognition.  Together with the native contact result, this
shows that structural information must be incorporated as contextual contact information and
evaluated under a split appropriate to the intended claim.

We also ran the released IRESfinder classifier over all 46,774 unique sequences using its original
Perl framed-k-mer extractor, released training data and feature indices, with only the Python 2.7
orchestration ported to the current scikit-learn API. Evaluation on the same ten repeated native
test folds yielded AUC/AUPR/F1 of 0.605074/0.273844/0.346292, reproducing the published rounded
values of 0.61/0.27/0.35. The author-provided 14-sequence example retained all labels and differed
in probability by at most 0.000190 from the released output.

An exact sequence audit showed that 695 of the 722 released IRESfinder training sequences occurred
in the IRES-LM benchmark, all from the 55k source, with 100\% label agreement. Removing these records
from every native test fold reduced mean AUC/AUPR/F1 from 0.605/0.274/0.346 to
0.602/0.267/0.337. The frozen component-disjoint test contained 55 exact training overlaps;
removing them yielded 0.602/0.197/0.309 versus 0.599/0.199/0.313 on the full cluster test. We
therefore disclose the contamination and report both views rather than treating IRESfinder as a
fully independent external model.

The released DeepCIP data provide a complementary same-sequence assay audit. Its three balanced
training subsets each contain 7,898 sequences and share the same 3,949 positives while subsampling
the negative pool; the balanced 1,164-sequence fixed test set has no exact overlap with any of the
three training subsets. However, 1,089 test sequences (93.6\%) occur exactly in the IRES-LM 55k
source. The DeepCIP circRNA-screen label and IRES-LM legacy label agree for only 46.7\% of these
identical sequences. Thus the released DeepCIP test is internally train-disjoint, yet it cannot be
treated as an interchangeable label view of the legacy benchmark.

Generalization weakened when an entire source was withheld. Training only on the 44,634-sequence
55k reporter library and testing on all 276 \texttt{IRESite\_exp} records reduced composition AUC to 0.600
(95\% bootstrap CI 0.533--0.668) and hashed k-mer AUC to 0.601 (0.537--0.664). AUPR was 0.694 and
0.681, respectively, against a held-out positive prevalence of 0.565. Thresholds selected only
within the 55k source gave low recall (0.199 and 0.179) and F1 (0.316 and 0.293). Thus even before
sequence-cluster evaluation, a complete source holdout exposes materially weaker portability than
the reconstructed mixed-source folds.

The exact 174-nt similarity graph contained 85,039 edges at $\geq90\%$ identity and 32,538
connected components; the two largest contained 3,318 and 3,202 sequences. Assigning complete
components yielded 31,249 training, 6,696 validation, and 6,696 test sequences with zero identity
edges crossing a split. On this frozen test set, composition achieved AUC 0.634 (95\% bootstrap CI
0.616--0.650), AUPR 0.230, and F1 0.336. The three-seed k-mer ensemble fell from its reconstructed
mixed-source AUC/AUPR of 0.723/0.452 to 0.578 (0.559--0.595)/0.250 (0.229--0.273), with F1 0.252.
The reversal of the apparent k-mer advantage indicates that near-neighbour structure materially
inflated the mixed-source random-fold estimate.

Applying the released IRESfinder model without refitting to the same component-disjoint test set
gave AUC 0.599, AUPR 0.199 and F1 0.313. Its AUC changed only modestly from the released repeated-fold
rerun, although its ranking remained below the composition control and its AUPR was only 0.035 above
the cluster-test positive prevalence of 0.165. This result separates robustness to the particular
near-neighbour split from strong absolute discrimination.

\subsection{Cross-assay transfer}
The normalized IRES-TrAPPr supplement contained 2,300 unique sequences, including 111 with an
unambiguous active call and 2,068 with an unambiguous inactive call. Only one sequence exactly
matched the legacy benchmark. Nevertheless, 2,048 sequences had a nearest legacy sequence at
$\geq90\%$ local-alignment identity and $\geq80\%$ coverage, and a 174-nt legacy fragment was
exactly contained in 1,405 direct-RNA constructs. This apparent overlap did not imply functional
agreement: the nearest legacy label agreed with the direct-RNA call for only 28.2\% of labelled
sequences, and agreement among exact-containment cases was 21.1\%.

Both frozen shortcut controls lost substantial discrimination and calibration under assay transfer
(Table~\ref{tab:assay-shift}; Figure~\ref{fig:assay-shift}). Composition features yielded AUC
0.579 and AUPR 0.058 on all 2,179 labelled direct-RNA sequences, only slightly above the 0.051
positive prevalence. The hashed 3--6-mer model reversed below random ranking (AUC 0.368; AUPR
0.037), despite reaching AUC 0.723 and AUPR 0.452 on reconstructed legacy folds. Restricting the
analysis to high-similarity sequences did not rescue transfer: composition fell to AUC 0.500 and
the k-mer model to 0.274. These results support treating the historical reporter score as
assay-specific rather than as a portable activity oracle.

The released IRESfinder model failed under the same zero-shot transfer despite reproducing its
native benchmark. Across all labelled direct-RNA records it achieved AUC 0.460 (95\% CI
0.422--0.496), AUPR 0.043 (0.041--0.047) against prevalence 0.051, and F1 0.081 at its released
0.5 threshold. Its calibration error was 0.518. On the high-similarity subset, AUC decreased to
0.393. Thus successful software reproduction on the legacy benchmark does not validate a model as
a direct-RNA activity oracle.

The released RNA-FM fold-0 checkpoint retained modest zero-shot rank signal on the direct-RNA set
(AUC 0.618, 95\% CI 0.582--0.652), but this did not generalize consistently across equally
native-performing checkpoints. Across all ten folds, frozen post hoc AUC varied from 0.251 to
0.821 and pairwise checkpoint rank correlations ranged from only 0.338 to 0.705. The predeclared
arithmetic-mean ten-checkpoint ensemble reached AUC 0.600 (0.567--0.632) and AUPR 0.058
(0.054--0.063), against prevalence 0.051. Earlier immutable five-, six-, seven- and
nine-checkpoint snapshots ranged from AUC 0.432 to 0.602, illustrating that out-of-assay ranking
is sensitive to checkpoint composition. At the released 0.5 threshold, the final ensemble
assigned every active record and 99.85\% of inactive records to the positive class, yielding F1
0.097, specificity 0.001 and ECE 0.906. On the high-similarity subset it reached AUC 0.577 and
AUPR 0.038. Thus neither the best post hoc fold nor fold 0 is a valid standalone transfer result.
The final aggregate uses every released checkpoint without selecting a fold, direction or
threshold on direct-RNA labels.

\subsection{Constrained full-length optimization}
We evaluated \textsc{StructIRES-Rank} over ten experimentally supported full-length IRES seeds.
For each of three deterministic pools, every parent contributed 512 unique length-preserving
substitution mutants with at most 3\% edited positions. Each arm retained 50 candidates per
parent from exactly the same pool, yielding 30 matched parent-by-run comparison units.

The sequence term is the released IRES-LM ensemble: the arithmetic mean of all ten RNA-FM and ten
UTR-LM released classifiers.  Its components reproduce the released native benchmark summaries
without using direct-RNA labels for checkpoint selection. On the shared mutation pools, IRES-LM
score-only optimization created substantial parent drift ($|\Delta\mathrm{MFE}|=4.021\pm1.254$
kcal/mol; pairing-profile $L_1=0.164\pm0.027$) and retained only $0.646\pm0.075$ of weighted
parent-derived high-confidence ensemble anchors. Energy ranking reduced MFE shift to
$0.647\pm0.196$; global ensemble ranking reduced pairing-profile distance to $0.055\pm0.014$;
and anchor ranking independently increased anchor retention to $0.920\pm0.028$. The combined
rank, \textsc{StructIRES-Rank}, retained all three properties simultaneously
($|\Delta\mathrm{MFE}|=0.666\pm0.226$; pairing-profile $L_1=0.037\pm0.010$; anchor retention
$0.955\pm0.015$) and improved structure-state identity from $0.798\pm0.048$ to
$0.954\pm0.049$ (Table~\ref{tab:design}; Figure~\ref{fig:structires-ablation}). These anchors
are thermodynamic ensemble features of the parent sequence, not experimentally mapped IRES motifs.

As an independent post-selection check, we fitted a low-capacity three-seed hashed 3--6-mer
direct-RNA proxy after excluding every S3-provenance record, and evaluated it once on the 222
held-out S3 records (AUC 0.592; AUPR 0.497). The proxy was never used for candidate generation,
rank selection or tuning. IRES-LM score-only candidates averaged 0.419 on this evaluator. Energy,
global ensemble, and anchor terms independently improved this value to 0.427, 0.431, and 0.431;
the combined \textsc{StructIRES-Rank} reached 0.435. Its paired gain over IRES-LM score-only was
0.0158 (95\% bootstrap CI 0.0079--0.0242), and its gains over the energy, ensemble, and anchor
ablations were 0.0086 (0.0035--0.0147), 0.0046 (0.0018--0.0080), and 0.0045
(0.0018--0.0080), respectively. This controlled ablation supports complementary roles for energy,
global ensemble, and parent-anchor constraints rather than an advantage attributable to an
increased query budget.

The public direct-RNA IAPV mutational scan provides a complementary, experimentally grounded
guardrail rather than another sequence predictor. Across 208 overlapping A-substitution tiles, 169
of 213 IAPV positions had direct measurement coverage. We converted the observed loss of IRES
translation at each position into a conservative edit-risk map, imputing the median measured risk
for unobserved positions. Adding this guardrail to the matched IAPV selection reduced mean MPRA
edit risk from $2.438\pm0.174$ to $1.782\pm0.087$ across the three frozen pools (paired difference
$-0.656$, bootstrap interval $-0.933$ to $-0.480$; Table~\ref{tab:iapv-mpra-guardrail}). This
reduction necessarily trades against a small amount of thermodynamic preservation
($|\Delta\mathrm{MFE}|$ rises from $0.641$ to $0.887$ kcal/mol), which is precisely why the
functional guardrail is reported as a separate, transparent ablation rather than folded into the
ten-seed headline result. It demonstrates that the framework can encode direct assay-derived
mutational knowledge, but does not establish activity for new sequences.

A reporter-only cargo-context stress test using NanoLuc, Firefly and mCherry contexts further
showed that constraint-guided designs had the lowest mean crosstalk ratio (0.163) and highest
mean context consistency (0.841) among the local and released score-only comparators. This is a
computational top-1 context stress test, not a translation assay. Collectively, all results are
computational structural and proxy metrics only; they establish candidate enrichment and
structure preservation, not improved IRES activity.

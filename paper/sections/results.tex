\section{Results}

\subsection{Dataset composition reveals a strong assay and length imbalance}
The assembled IRES-AI identification set contains 46,774 unique sequences, of which 9,172 are
positive and 37,602 are negative. Of these, 44,641 sequences are exactly 174 nt. Figure~\ref{fig:overview}
places this legacy benchmark alongside the direct-RNA and structure/context evidence used by the
primary task.

The released long-form split is not a mutually exclusive ten-fold partition: 16,176 sequences
never occur in a test set and 12,294 occur in two or more test sets. We therefore separate external
published values from a deterministic reconstructed-fold pilot and reserve similarity-cluster
evaluation for the locked benchmark.

\subsection{Identification benchmark}
On reconstructed nested folds, composition features already achieved AUC $0.710\pm0.009$ and AUPR
$0.405\pm0.015$, while hashed 3--6-mer features achieved AUC $0.723\pm0.011$ and AUPR
$0.452\pm0.016$. These are shortcut-control pilots rather than claims against published models.
Published reference values and project runs remain visibly separated in
Table~\ref{tab:prediction} and Figure~\ref{fig:prediction-pilot}.

We independently recomputed the released ten-fold CSV aggregates and recovered RNA-FM
AUC/AUPR/F1 of 0.779/0.615/0.506 and UTR-LM values of 0.765/0.581/0.493. We then executed all ten
released UTR-LM checkpoints on their native test folds and obtained 0.764938/0.580823/0.492983;
the maximum fold-level absolute deviation from the released CSV was $3.74\times10^{-6}$. The first
verified released RNA-FM checkpoint was then rerun on its 4,678-record native test fold, giving
AUC 0.777114, AUPR 0.594668 and F1 0.524311. Four additional valid checkpoints (folds 1, 2, 6 and
9) were subsequently rerun, followed by fold 5. Across the six available folds, the interim mean
and standard deviation were 0.777740$\pm$0.011009, 0.611767$\pm$0.016782 and
0.519049$\pm$0.023373. We label this explicitly as a 6/10-checkpoint operational result; the final
aggregate awaits the remaining large checkpoint downloads. Source inspection further showed that both
released training scripts evaluate the test set after every epoch and select the saved checkpoint
using test AUPR. We therefore label these values as released-protocol references rather than
independent blind-test estimates; corrected reruns reserve validation data for model selection.

We also ran the released IRESfinder classifier over all 46,774 unique sequences using its original
Perl framed-k-mer extractor, released training data and feature indices, with only the Python 2.7
orchestration ported to the current scikit-learn API. Evaluation on the same ten repeated native
test folds yielded AUC/AUPR/F1 of 0.605074/0.273844/0.346292, reproducing the published rounded
values of 0.61/0.27/0.35. The author-provided 14-sequence example retained all labels and differed
in probability by at most 0.000190 from the released output.

An exact sequence audit showed that 695 of the 722 released IRESfinder training sequences occurred
in the IRES-LM benchmark, all from the 55k source, with 100\% label agreement. Removing these records
from every native test fold reduced mean AUC/AUPR/F1 from 0.605/0.274/0.346 to
0.602/0.267/0.337. The frozen component-disjoint test contained 55 exact training overlaps;
removing them yielded 0.602/0.197/0.309 versus 0.599/0.199/0.313 on the full cluster test. We
therefore disclose the contamination and report both views rather than treating IRESfinder as a
fully independent external model.

The released DeepCIP data provide a complementary same-sequence assay audit. Its three balanced
training subsets each contain 7,898 sequences and share the same 3,949 positives while subsampling
the negative pool; the balanced 1,164-sequence fixed test set has no exact overlap with any of the
three training subsets. However, 1,089 test sequences (93.6\%) occur exactly in the IRES-LM 55k
source. The DeepCIP circRNA-screen label and IRES-LM legacy label agree for only 46.7\% of these
identical sequences. Thus the released DeepCIP test is internally train-disjoint, yet it cannot be
treated as an interchangeable label view of the legacy benchmark.

Generalization weakened when an entire source was withheld. Training only on the 44,634-sequence
55k reporter library and testing on all 276 \texttt{IRESite\_exp} records reduced composition AUC to 0.600
(95\% bootstrap CI 0.533--0.668) and hashed k-mer AUC to 0.601 (0.537--0.664). AUPR was 0.694 and
0.681, respectively, against a held-out positive prevalence of 0.565. Thresholds selected only
within the 55k source gave low recall (0.199 and 0.179) and F1 (0.316 and 0.293). Thus even before
sequence-cluster evaluation, a complete source holdout exposes materially weaker portability than
the reconstructed mixed-source folds.

The exact 174-nt similarity graph contained 85,039 edges at $\geq90\%$ identity and 32,538
connected components; the two largest contained 3,318 and 3,202 sequences. Assigning complete
components yielded 31,249 training, 6,696 validation, and 6,696 test sequences with zero identity
edges crossing a split. On this frozen test set, composition achieved AUC 0.634 (95\% bootstrap CI
0.616--0.650), AUPR 0.230, and F1 0.336. The three-seed k-mer ensemble fell from its reconstructed
mixed-source AUC/AUPR of 0.723/0.452 to 0.578 (0.559--0.595)/0.250 (0.229--0.273), with F1 0.252.
The reversal of the apparent k-mer advantage indicates that near-neighbour structure materially
inflated the mixed-source random-fold estimate.

Applying the released IRESfinder model without refitting to the same component-disjoint test set
gave AUC 0.599, AUPR 0.199 and F1 0.313. Its AUC changed only modestly from the released repeated-fold
rerun, although its ranking remained below the composition control and its AUPR was only 0.035 above
the cluster-test positive prevalence of 0.165. This result separates robustness to the particular
near-neighbour split from strong absolute discrimination.

\subsection{Cross-assay transfer}
The normalized IRES-TrAPPr supplement contained 2,300 unique sequences, including 111 with an
unambiguous active call and 2,068 with an unambiguous inactive call. Only one sequence exactly
matched the legacy benchmark. Nevertheless, 2,048 sequences had a nearest legacy sequence at
$\geq90\%$ local-alignment identity and $\geq80\%$ coverage, and a 174-nt legacy fragment was
exactly contained in 1,405 direct-RNA constructs. This apparent overlap did not imply functional
agreement: the nearest legacy label agreed with the direct-RNA call for only 28.2\% of labelled
sequences, and agreement among exact-containment cases was 21.1\%.

Both frozen shortcut controls lost substantial discrimination and calibration under assay transfer
(Table~\ref{tab:assay-shift}; Figure~\ref{fig:assay-shift}). Composition features yielded AUC
0.579 and AUPR 0.058 on all 2,179 labelled direct-RNA sequences, only slightly above the 0.051
positive prevalence. The hashed 3--6-mer model reversed below random ranking (AUC 0.368; AUPR
0.037), despite reaching AUC 0.723 and AUPR 0.452 on reconstructed legacy folds. Restricting the
analysis to high-similarity sequences did not rescue transfer: composition fell to AUC 0.500 and
the k-mer model to 0.274. These results support treating the historical reporter score as
assay-specific rather than as a portable activity oracle.

The released IRESfinder model failed under the same zero-shot transfer despite reproducing its
native benchmark. Across all labelled direct-RNA records it achieved AUC 0.460 (95\% CI
0.422--0.496), AUPR 0.043 (0.041--0.047) against prevalence 0.051, and F1 0.081 at its released
0.5 threshold. Its calibration error was 0.518. On the high-similarity subset, AUC decreased to
0.393. Thus successful software reproduction on the legacy benchmark does not validate a model as
a direct-RNA activity oracle.

The released RNA-FM fold-0 checkpoint retained modest zero-shot rank signal on the direct-RNA set
(AUC 0.618, 95\% CI 0.582--0.652), but this did not generalize across equally native-performing
checkpoints. Across folds 0, 1, 2, 5, 6 and 9, AUC varied from 0.251 to 0.641 and pairwise checkpoint
rank correlations ranged from only 0.440 to 0.705. The arithmetic-mean six-checkpoint ensemble
reached AUC 0.583 (0.549--0.616) and AUPR 0.055 (0.052--0.061), against prevalence 0.051. Its
interim five-checkpoint predecessor without fold 5 had reached only 0.432/0.041, illustrating that
out-of-assay ranking is highly sensitive to checkpoint composition. At the released 0.5 threshold,
the six-checkpoint ensemble assigned every active record and 99.7\% of inactive records to the
positive class, yielding F1 0.097, specificity 0.003 and ECE 0.908. On the high-similarity subset
it reached AUC 0.585 and AUPR 0.038. Thus selecting fold 0 alone would materially overstate the
stability of transfer even though the current partial ensemble has above-random ranking. The final
analysis will repeat the
predeclared arithmetic ensemble after all ten checkpoints are available, without selecting a fold,
direction or threshold on direct-RNA labels.

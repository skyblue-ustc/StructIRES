\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Motivation:} Learning-based IRES design commonly optimizes a sequence score alone. For
full-length IRESes, this can select mutations that increase a model score while disrupting the
parent energy landscape and structural ensemble, and reporter-trained scores may not transfer to
direct-RNA settings. We address this objective-misalignment problem with constraint-guided IRES
optimization rather than treating a legacy classifier as a complete design oracle.
\textbf{Results:} We introduce \textsc{StructIRES-Rank}, an assay-aware constrained rank-selection
layer that combines a released IRES recognition score with parent-relative minimum-free-energy and
ensemble-pairing preservation. Across three frozen 512-candidate mutation pools for each of ten
experimentally supported full-length IRES seeds, all four arms receive identical candidate sets and
selection budgets. Relative to the exactly reproduced release-compatible RNA-FM fold-0 score-only
baseline, energy-only and ensemble-only constraints improve the held-out direct-RNA proxy from
0.419 to 0.428, while their combination reaches 0.433 (paired gain 0.0143, 95\% bootstrap CI
0.0074--0.0220). \textsc{StructIRES-Rank} also reduces absolute MFE deviation from
3.899 to 0.575 kcal/mol and pairing-profile distance from 0.161 to 0.050. Reproduced legacy
predictor results and cross-assay tests serve as a design stress test: strong in-assay
classification alone is insufficient for robust full-length optimization. Our results establish
computational candidate enrichment and structural preservation, not experimentally verified
translation improvement.

\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Motivation:} Learning-based IRES design commonly optimizes a sequence score alone. For
full-length IRESes, this can select mutations that increase a model score while disrupting the
parent energy landscape and structural ensemble, and reporter-trained scores may not transfer to
direct-RNA settings. We address this objective-misalignment problem with constraint-guided IRES
optimization rather than treating a legacy classifier as a complete design oracle.
\textbf{Results:} We introduce an assay-aware, generator-agnostic framework that ranks candidates
by sequence plausibility while explicitly preserving parent-relative minimum free energy and
ensemble pairing profiles. The framework is evaluated on three frozen 512-candidate mutation pools
for each of ten experimentally supported full-length IRES seeds, so random, local score-only,
released RNA-FM/UTR-LM score-only, structure-only, and robust-full selection receive identical
candidate sets and query budgets. Relative to local score-only selection, robust-full reduced
absolute MFE deviation from 3.236 to 0.567 kcal/mol (82.5\%) and pairing-profile deviation from
0.147 to 0.048 (67.2\%), while retaining a small sequence-plausibility trade-off. On a completely
held-out S3 direct-RNA computational proxy, robust-full increased the mean candidate score by
0.0110 over local score-only and by 0.0142 over the exactly reproduced released RNA-FM fold-0
score-only baseline; both paired 95\% bootstrap intervals excluded zero. In contrast, released
RNA-FM score-only selection was indistinguishable from random mutation on this independent proxy
and showed substantial structural drift. Reproduced legacy predictor results and cross-assay tests
therefore serve as a design stress test: strong in-assay classification alone is insufficient for
robust full-length optimization. Our results establish computational candidate enrichment and
structural preservation, not experimentally verified translation improvement.

\end{abstract}
